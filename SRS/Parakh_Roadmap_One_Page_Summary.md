# Parakh — Execution, Accountability & Evidence Roadmap
### One-page summary · 16 August – 10 October 2026

**Thesis.** Most civic platforms digitize the complaint. Parakh is designed to digitize the proof. A complaint is an assertion; evidence is a record another person can check later.

**Status, stated plainly.** Parakh is in **early prototype development**. The private repository, development environment, Flutter installation, Android toolchain and a starting citizen-app scaffold exist, and initial scaffold checks passed. **The functional prototype is not built. Parakh is not deployed. No government, NGO, UNESCO or UN partnership is signed. No adoption or approval has occurred. No social impact has been demonstrated.** Scaffold tests passing means the toolchain works — it does not mean the Parakh workflow has been tested. Punjab is the starting context because of language, relationships and local understanding, not because province-wide deployment has been approved.

**Four conflicts between this roadmap and the approved SRS, named rather than silently resolved.** The five-role workflow (UC Officer, Contractor, Mayor & Operations) is classified **Future — not built** in SRS §3.4; Release 0 has four actors plus an audited proxy capability. The public heatmap is **P2**. AI duplicate and similarity detection is **P2, research-only** — P0 AI is transcription only. Docker deployment is **blocked** on TBD-049 and TBD-050. Where the deck and the SRS differ, **the SRS governs** until Hassan records a decision.

**Release 0 target, 10 October 2026.** 84 P0 functional requirements, four actors, synthetic data only. Must demonstrate: citizen report creation; Urdu/English foundation; voice, text and media capture; location and timestamp; evidence preservation and integrity checks; privacy-preserving reporter identifier; human moderation; Street Representative verification; role-based access control; status history and audit trail; accessibility and low-bandwidth basics; automated tests; a recorded end-to-end demonstration. Explicitly out of scope: public launch, live government integration, procurement, contractor selection, payments, AI final decisions, facial recognition, reporter-identity disclosure, unsupervised testing with minors, impact claims from a demonstration.

**Non-negotiable boundaries.** Reporter identity is absent from Representative-facing queries at the query level, enforced by a build-failing schema test. AI transcribes and annotates; it never accepts, rejects, verifies, prioritises or closes — enforced at the database-grant level. Minors are excluded by configuration and mandatory adult attestation. Evidence and audit records are append-only. If the deadline is at risk, **features are cut before testing, privacy, evidence integrity, documentation or human review**.

**Ownership.** Hassan owns concept, product definition, scope, priorities, acceptance criteria, community research, Urdu/English language, the Development Journal, the evidence archive and all external claims — and records his own code contributions file by file. The developer owns implementation, migrations, API contracts, permissions, evidence hashing, tests, Docker configuration and technical documentation, commits under his own GitHub identity, and must flag any requirement that is ambiguous, unsafe or unrealistic. **Hassan does not claim code the developer wrote.**

**Timeline.** 16–18 Aug governance and scope freeze · 19–25 Aug UX and clickable workflow · 26 Aug–1 Sep citizen vertical slice · 2–8 Sep evidence and human-review layer · 9–15 Sep government workflow · 16–22 Sep contractor and operations workflow, first internal end-to-end demo · 23–28 Sep security, usability, deployment · 29 Sep–3 Oct controlled validation with consenting adults or synthetic cases · 4–7 Oct release candidate · 8–10 Oct final release and evidence package.

**Engagement, three stages that each earn the next.** 25–30 Aug: informal workflow validation through an existing contact — feedback, not partnership. 15–22 Sep: a 15–20 minute prototype feedback meeting; ask for introductions to the operational department and PITB, never for an adoption announcement, equipment or money. 5–10 Oct: a technical-review request with the brief, pitch, demo, architecture, privacy note, pilot boundaries, test results and known limitations, proposing a limited supervised non-public pilot subject to written approval. Institutions are chosen by mandate fit, not prestige; UNESCO is relevant only where Parakh genuinely meets its mandate, and no logo or claim of support may be used without a written relationship.

**Measurement.** Outputs, outcomes and impact are separate. Every metric carries a Target and an Actual column, and **Actual stays empty until it is measured**. Impact cannot be claimed from an early prototype or a small usability test.

**Definition of done.** Fifteen conditions. If any is unmet on 10 October, the project is described as a **"prototype in development"**, not a completed prototype. Currently outside the team's control: `R-6` and `R-7` are unapproved, so the 84-requirement scope and 18-route count are proposed rather than frozen; `TBD-049` and `TBD-050` block deployment; ten decisions block field testing; all eighteen readiness-gate rows are unsigned.
